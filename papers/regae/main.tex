\begin{abstract}
Regenerative Autoencoders (ReGAE) are a family of models for generating data and regenerating it after perturbation through repeated denoising. In the conditional form, an encoder compresses a sample into a latent tensor and a denoiser replaces the conventional decoder. The denoiser preserves the shape of its input, allowing its output to become the next state: generation starts from noise, while regeneration resumes from a disturbed reconstruction. This formulation extends the regenerative motivation of Neural Cellular Automata Manifold. An unconditional variant omits the encoder and latent conditioning, using the denoiser as a generative model over data or learned representations. The same design can be instantiated for different data dimensionalities, network architectures, and reconstruction objectives. We investigate what such models must learn to represent both samples and relationships within a dataset. As a proof of concept, a conditional ReGAE learns image patches from Pandora's Cluster and an unconditional ReGAE models their spatial latent structure. This application illustrates the broader construction without defining its scope. We discuss the existing training evidence and qualitative observations, and connect the framework to diffusion autoencoders, denoising dynamics, recursive modeling, and compression.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
ReGAE organizes generation and regeneration around an iteratively reusable denoiser with matching input and output shapes. An encoder supplies a compressed representation in the conditional form; removing that conditioning gives an unconditional member of the same family. Architecture, loss, data dimensionality, and hierarchical organization remain choices of the application. Pandora's Cluster illustrates one such choice: a conditional model learns image samples, and an unconditional model learns spatial relationships among their representations. The broader research question is how reusable regenerative operators can capture different aspects of a dataset, from individual samples to the structure connecting them. The existing implementation offers a concrete starting point, while the extent and reliability of regeneration across applications remain empirical questions.
