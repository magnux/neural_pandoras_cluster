\begin{abstract}
Denoising models learn to correct corrupted data toward a desired state. Neural cellular automata use the biological term \emph{regeneration} for recovery after an already developed pattern is damaged. Building on this viewpoint and the autoencoding lineage of Neural Cellular Automata Manifold, we present Regenerative Autoencoders (ReGAE): a family of generative denoising models that directly predict corrected states and reuse their outputs as subsequent inputs. An encoder supplies a latent representation in the conditional form; an unconditional variant retains the denoiser without that code. Neither variant requires an iteration index or noise-level input. For fixed conditioning, one learned correction map therefore serves both generation from noise and renewed restoration after perturbation. We interpret this construction geometrically as a time-independent state update, and compare it with time-indexed diffusion and flow models while distinguishing state, noise, and velocity prediction. The resulting landscape intuition does not assume a learned scalar energy or guaranteed convergence. A proof of concept on Pandora's Cluster composes two ReGAE instances to learn image samples and spatial relationships among their representations. The existing evidence motivates a broader discussion of dataset modeling, recursive architectures, and compression.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
ReGAE combines the autoencoding lineage of NCAM with the regeneration objective associated with NCA: after a state is perturbed, the same learned process can be applied to restore it. Its denoiser predicts the desired state directly, with matching input and output shapes and no required noise-level or timestep input. This yields one learned map per fixed code, whose induced displacement field provides a precise interpretation of the landscape intuition. Diffusion and flow models offer closely related descriptions of motion in sample space, but prediction targets and sampling rules must be compared separately. Pandora's Cluster illustrates how conditional and unconditional ReGAE instances can model samples and relationships among their representations. The broader question is how such reusable correction maps capture different aspects of a dataset, and under what conditions their trajectories provide reliable generation and repair.
