\begin{abstract}
What must a model learn to reconstruct a dataset and the relationships within it? We investigate this question through Regenerative Autoencoders (ReGAE), a family of models that makes reconstruction a reusable denoising process. Neural cellular automata call recovery after damage \emph{regeneration}; ReGAE adopts this objective within the autoencoding lineage of Neural Cellular Automata Manifold. A denoiser directly predicts a corrected state without timestep conditioning, allowing the same update interface to generate from noise and resume reconstruction after perturbation. A conditional instance uses an encoder-derived code; an unconditional instance can model the resulting representations. This composition makes part of a system's own learned structure the subject of further learning. We present a two-level implementation on Pandora's Cluster, where an image autoencoder represents local patches and a latent denoiser learns spatial relationships through displaced prediction. The archived training records and reported synthesis behavior provide an exploratory account of this construction. The aim is to study recursive representation modeling rather than optimize a particular task benchmark. We frame the implemented hierarchy as representation-level self-modeling and examine its connection to the broader hypothesis that modeling representations, computation, and learning could contribute to more general adaptive intelligence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
ReGAE treats reconstruction as a process that can be reused: on a noise state, on a damaged reconstruction, and on representations produced by another model. Its direct, time-unconditioned denoising interface provides the common operation. The Pandora implementation makes the composition concrete by learning patch representations and then modeling their spatial structure with another member of the same family. This is a representation-level step toward the broader goal of systems that model what they have learned and eventually how they learn. The contribution is the inspectable construction and the questions it exposes about retained information, relationships, and recursive learning. Establishing how far such recursion supports adaptive intelligence remains an open research direction.
