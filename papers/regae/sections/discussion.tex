\section{Discussion: What Has Been Learned?}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Recursive meta-modeling as the research objective}
\label{sec:recursive-discussion}

The purpose of ReGAE is to investigate how a system can internalize data through successive acts of modeling. A first model learns a representation through reconstruction. A subsequent model treats the resulting representations as data, learning structure that was not specified by the first reconstruction objective. We call this \emph{meta-modeling}: the object of the higher-level model is the structure expressed in the lower-level model's representations. The repeated modeling interface is the organizing idea; the astronomical-image task makes it concrete.

The self-reference belongs to the composite system. The Gencoder learns a field produced by its own encoder subsystem, so it models part of the system's internal representation of the data. This is the representation-level sense of \emph{modeling itself} used here. The same denoising construction operates on pixels and on codes, with different parameters and objectives. The implemented hierarchy therefore realizes a concrete meta-modeling step: information represented by one component becomes the subject of another component's model. Its object is the learned code field, rather than the lower model's complete computation or parameter set.

The broader hypothesis is that recursively modeling such internal structures could reveal increasingly rich relationships in the data. A model of representations can itself provide representations whose relationships become candidates for further modeling. This suggests a route toward systems that build and use models of their own modeled world, giving meta-modeling a potential role in the pursuit of artificial general intelligence. In this account, understanding means the ability to reconstruct represented content and use its learned relationships to predict or generate further states. The current two-level case makes the first such composition explicit; it does not establish that further levels necessarily capture all information or are sufficient for general intelligence.

\subsection{What additional levels can capture}

The Pandora decomposition separates information carried by individual codes from dependencies among codes. Keeping the original code arrangement supplies those dependencies externally. Learning a model of that arrangement instead places some of its regularities in the Gencoder's parameters. The reported coherent views are consistent with that intended role; apparent relocations also expose the ambiguity of content-based state updates without a global source address.

Further modeling can reveal dependencies in what has been retained, but it cannot guarantee recovery of everything in the original data. If an encoder maps two samples to the same code, a higher-level model given only that code cannot distinguish them without additional context or information. A sequence of lossy representations therefore needs explicit questions about what is preserved at each level. Neither the hierarchy's depth nor visual resemblance alone demonstrates complete dataset understanding.

\subsection{A connection to compression}
\label{sec:compression}

This viewpoint also connects to compression: shared weights describe regularities, sample codes retain variation, and another model can describe relationships among those codes. A minimum-description-length account must count model and data terms together~\cite{mdl}. Prediction and explicit coding can be closely connected~\cite{lmcompression}, but a useful generative hierarchy is not automatically an efficient codec. Actual image compression also needs a coding procedure, precision choices, and a rate--distortion comparison~\cite{coin}. Appendix~\ref{sec:storage} accounts for the archived parameter and latent payloads. The broader intelligence-as-compression hypothesis is a separate interpretive question.

\subsection{Scope of the present account}

The available evidence concerns one two-dimensional case with separately trained representation levels. Training traces establish progress on the implemented objectives; the reported sampling behavior has not yet been accompanied here by the retained media. There is no controlled repair evaluation or comparison of additional meta-modeling levels. These limits determine the strength of the empirical conclusions, while the formulation and code make the proposed construction inspectable.

The next empirical questions follow directly from the design: which relationships each level captures, what information is lost, and when repeated correction repairs or changes a state. The present paper preserves the original exploratory work and its motivation. Its further development can build on existing run artifacts, without making new experiments a prerequisite for documenting the idea.
