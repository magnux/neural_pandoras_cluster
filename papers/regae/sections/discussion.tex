\section{Discussion: What Has Been Learned?}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What it means for a system to model itself}
\label{sec:recursive-discussion}

In the patch model, each code is useful to the extent that the decoder can use it to recover the corresponding patch. Two codes might often occur beside one another without the decoder ever having to predict that fact: it sees only one code at a time. The relationship becomes learnable by the Gencoder because its training examples keep neighboring codes together and its target asks how the entire neighborhood changes under a shift. In this sense, what the first model produces determines the subject matter of the second, while the choice of training pairs determines the question the second model is asked to answer.

This is the sense in which the system \emph{models itself}. The encoder and the Gencoder belong to one system; the latter models a field of representations made by the former. The Gencoder does not observe the encoder's weights or its intermediate computations. Nor would merely applying one denoiser many times constitute meta-modeling: that repeats a learned operation on a state. Meta-modeling here consists in training another model on relationships among the codes the encoder produces.

Our longer-term hypothesis is that recursion of this kind could contribute to artificial general intelligence. For a third level to be useful, there would have to be a relationship available across second-level representations that its training objective rewards it for learning. In the mosaic, for example, one could ask whether a sequence of views disambiguates locations that look alike in any single view. That would give a further level a specific prediction to make. Only the patch model and the Gencoder were trained here.

\subsection{Which information survives?}

An ordered grid of patch codes and a model that predicts plausible code neighborhoods serve different purposes. The grid records which patch occurred at each indexed location. The Gencoder can learn regularities in that arrangement without storing a unique address for every patch. Where two regions look alike, their likely continuations may also be plausible alternatives to each other. If a sampled trajectory follows the other continuation, it can preserve local coherence while losing correspondence with the source. This offers one explanation for the reported relocations.

There is also a limit set by the encoder. If two distinct patches map to the same code, no model given only that code can determine which patch was encoded. Neighboring codes may provide the missing clue, but only if those neighbors retain distinguishing information. Additional levels can make retained relationships usable; adding a level is no guarantee that lost distinctions can be recovered.

\subsection{A connection to compression}
\label{sec:compression}

In the patch autoencoder, the weights describe what reconstructions have in common and the codes carry information that varies from patch to patch. A model of the codes can describe regularities that would otherwise have to be recorded in their arrangement. This suggests a compression question: after paying for the model, how much information must still be transmitted to recover this particular image at a specified fidelity? Minimum description length counts the model and the data together~\cite{mdl}. A predictor can support an explicit coding scheme~\cite{lmcompression}, but the Gencoder sampler does not specify how to encode the difference between its prediction and the actual mosaic. Appendix~\ref{sec:storage} reports parameter and code sizes; a codec comparison would also need a bitstream and a measured reconstruction error~\cite{coin}.

\subsection{What the experiment establishes}

The notebooks document a two-level construction and training losses for both the pixel and code objectives. The trajectories described in Sec.~\ref{sec:evidence} are the author's observations from the original runs; the corresponding frames are not in the archive. The loss curves therefore answer a narrower question than the visual account. They do not tell us how closely a trajectory follows the source, or how much perturbation the decoder can undo. In particular, the final video routine calls the image decoder once per patch while feeding predictions back at the latent level. It illustrates the Gencoder's role in synthesis; it does not measure pixel-space regeneration.
