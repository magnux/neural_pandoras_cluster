\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Regeneration, dynamics, and ambiguous continuations}
The Gencoder is trained to map a corrupted observation of one spatial window to a clean observation of a nearby window. It is consequently both a restoration operator and a predictor. With elementwise $L_1$ training, an optimal unconstrained predictor would return coordinatewise medians of $z^+$ conditional on the corrupted input. This observation helps explain why one-step regression need not reproduce the full distribution of possible continuations. Injected noise makes the sampled transition stochastic, but does not by itself make its transition probabilities equal to those of the source image.

Writing the full canvas update as
\begin{equation}
  S_{t+1}=F_\psi(S_t,\eta_t;t)
  \label{eq:canvas-dynamics}
\end{equation}
makes its dynamical character explicit. The explicit $t$ dependence comes from the refresh schedule, and $\eta_t$ collects the noise used across windows. Setting the noise draws to zero defines a formal displacement field $F_\psi(S,0;t)-S$; for a nonlinear network, this generally differs from the expected displacement under random noise. Treating either field as a continuous-time derivative would define a new model: the implementation neither trains a small-step limit nor uses an ODE integrator. Similarly, the losses in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:regae-ae-loss} and~\eqref{eq:regae-gencoder-loss} do not establish the score needed for a probability-flow ODE~\cite{sde}. The connection to diffusion is the use of learned denoising and latent generative structure, with materially different objectives and sampling rules.

The absence of global coordinates makes continuation depend on recognizable content. A unique local configuration can provide a strong cue to the next view; several similar configurations can support competing continuations. This offers a useful interpretation of reported jumps without requiring the network to maintain an explicit location variable. Such jumps could also arise from accumulated prediction error or departure from the training distribution. The current observations cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

\subsection{Multiplicative gain and the ``gravity'' analogy}
The gravity branch scales a feature field pointwise. Multipliers above one amplify a feature and those below one attenuate it; spatial interactions elsewhere in the network determine how this affects an image. The analogy is an intuitive name for learned gain, not a physical gravitational law or an explicit transport of mass between pixels.

The unbounded exponential gate in v0 motivated a memorable development observation. The author recalls bright stars becoming progressively brighter, followed by large dark regions, termed ``neural black holes.'' The relevant checkpoints and recordings were not retained, so the event is an anecdote rather than a reproducible result. Exponential gain offers a possible mechanism for amplification: in an isolated gate-only recurrence $h_{t+1}=h_t\exp(g_t)$, one obtains $h_T=h_0\exp(\sum_{t<T}g_t)$. This toy calculation does not model the remaining transformations or identify the cause of the reported darkening. In particular, the code supplies no verified overflow-triggered reset mechanism. The bounded v2 gate removes unbounded growth from this individual operation, while leaving stability of the complete recurrence unresolved.

\subsection{Architectural reuse across levels}
Reusing the decoder family for latent prediction expresses a simple hypothesis: a useful reconstruction operator for observable fields may also model fields of learned representations. The present hierarchy instantiates this idea at two levels. The networks have different parameters, dimensions, and conditioning, and the higher level models encoder outputs rather than the lower network's parameter dynamics.

This is a motivation for recursively extensible architectures, not a demonstration that a network has learned its own learning algorithm. No task distribution, meta-optimization objective, or self-modifying training procedure is evaluated here. Further levels could be defined by repeating the representation-and-prediction construction, but their effectiveness is untested. The value of the current experiment is more concrete: the same building blocks can express both image reconstruction and spatial prediction among image codes.

\subsection{What is compressed?}
\label{sec:compression}
A $32\times32$ RGB patch contains 3,072 scalar channel values and is represented by 256 latent scalars: a factor of 12 in scalar count. At 8 bits per RGB channel and 32 bits per latent scalar, the nominal payload reduction is instead a factor of three, before storing a decoder or accounting for reconstruction distortion. Numerical precision is therefore part of the argument, not a secondary implementation detail.

Table~\ref{tab:storage} separates several storage quantities using decimal megabytes. These are calculations from dimensions and parameter counts, not measurements of compressed files. The actual crop lattice also has boundary and rounding conventions, so a single code grid is not itself a specified exact codec for every source pixel.

The hierarchy suggests a useful conceptual division. The first stage shares a model of appearance across many patches; a spatial code grid then specifies where those appearances occur. The second stage learns regularities in that grid. It can therefore reduce the amount of spatial information that must be supplied to generate plausible content. It does not follow that it has replaced the exact grid by a lossless description: recovering a particular source arrangement could still require an initial state, a trajectory specification, and residual information.

A minimum-description-length account would include both model and data terms~\cite{mdl}. For a practical lossy codec, the relevant object is a bitstream together with a reconstruction distortion, including the cost of any non-shared decoder. ReGAE specifies neither entropy coding nor quantized model storage nor a rate--distortion evaluation. The reported large checkpoint sizes may also include optimizer tensors: AMSGrad stores three moment-related tensors per parameter in addition to the weights. This can bring FP32 training checkpoints near the author's recalled 0.89 GB and 1.4 GB without implying that inference requires that storage. A broader claim that intelligence is compression remains an interpretive position; this experiment provides a concrete example of progressively modeling local and relational information, rather than a test of that claim.

\subsection{Limitations and next documentation steps}
The study uses one image and lacks controlled ablations, held-out evaluation, and comparisons at matched capacity or compute. Its fixed perceptual objective draws on externally pretrained features. One-step training does not explicitly optimize long-horizon behavior or establish recurrent stability, and uncertainty is not calibrated. Historical notebook inconsistencies also limit immediate end-to-end reproduction. These boundaries are part of the scope of this first report.

The immediate next steps are documentary: attach existing generated examples, identify their checkpoints and sampler settings, and align descriptions with the author's records. The related Neural Vapor Deposition work concerns progressive parameter growth and removal and is reserved for a separate manuscript. Neither that study nor new ReGAE training is needed to state the method presented here. Quantitative evaluation and larger recursive hierarchies remain possible future research directions.
