\section{Discussion: What Has Been Learned?}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Recursive modeling as the research objective}
\label{sec:recursive-discussion}

The purpose of ReGAE is to investigate how a system can internalize data through successive acts of modeling. A first model learns a representation through reconstruction. A subsequent model treats the resulting representations as data, learning structure that was not specified by the first reconstruction objective. The repeated modeling interface is the organizing idea; the astronomical-image task makes it concrete. In this account, understanding is operational: the system can reconstruct represented content and use learned relationships to predict or generate further states.

The self-reference belongs to the composite system. The Gencoder learns a field produced by its own encoder subsystem, so it models part of the system's internal representation of the data. This is the representation-level sense of \emph{modeling itself} used here. The same denoising construction is used on pixels and on codes, with different parameters and objectives. It supplies a concrete starting point for a recursively extensible architecture, rather than merely a larger network devoted to one image-generation task.

The motivating hypothesis is that such recursion could eventually extend from representations to models of computation and learning. A system that could represent, predict, and revise how it learns might use its experience more generally. We regard that possibility as relevant to the pursuit of artificial general intelligence. The present contribution makes the representation-level step explicit; it does not establish that this step is sufficient for general intelligence or that increasing the number of levels necessarily improves understanding.

This ambition connects to meta-learning, but the object of learning must be specified. Meta-learning commonly concerns improving the learning procedure through experience over learning episodes~\cite{hospedales2022}; it can include both single-task and multi-task settings. MAML, for example, trains an initialization for effective adaptation after a small number of updates~\cite{finn2017}. The current Gencoder learns relationships among encoder outputs. It neither predicts the encoder's parameter updates nor optimizes a learning procedure. We therefore describe the experiment as representation-level self-modeling and an architectural motivation for meta-learning, rather than a demonstration of learning-to-learn. Extending the object being modeled from representations to the learning process would be a substantive further step.

\subsection{What additional levels can capture}

The Pandora decomposition separates information carried by individual codes from dependencies among codes. Keeping the original code arrangement supplies those dependencies externally. Learning a model of that arrangement instead places some of its regularities in the Gencoder's parameters. The reported coherent views are consistent with that intended role; apparent relocations also expose the ambiguity of content-based state updates without a global source address.

Further modeling can reveal dependencies in what has been retained, but it cannot guarantee recovery of everything in the original data. If an encoder maps two samples to the same code, a higher-level model given only that code cannot distinguish them without additional context or information. A sequence of lossy representations therefore needs explicit questions about what is preserved at each level. Neither the hierarchy's depth nor visual resemblance alone demonstrates complete dataset understanding.

\subsection{A connection to compression}
\label{sec:compression}

This viewpoint also connects to compression: shared weights describe regularities, sample codes retain variation, and another model can describe relationships among those codes. A minimum-description-length account must count model and data terms together~\cite{mdl}. Prediction and explicit coding can be closely connected~\cite{lmcompression}, but a useful generative hierarchy is not automatically an efficient codec. Actual image compression also needs a coding procedure, precision choices, and a rate--distortion comparison~\cite{coin}. Appendix~\ref{sec:storage} accounts for the archived parameter and latent payloads. The broader intelligence-as-compression hypothesis is a separate interpretive question.

\subsection{Scope of the present account}

The available evidence concerns one two-dimensional case with separately trained representation levels. Training traces establish progress on the implemented objectives; the reported sampling behavior has not yet been accompanied here by the retained media. There is no controlled repair evaluation, comparison of hierarchy depths, or measurement of improved learning across episodes. These limits determine the strength of the empirical conclusions, while the formulation and code make the proposed construction inspectable.

The next empirical questions follow directly from the design: which relationships each level captures, what information is lost, and when repeated correction repairs or changes a state. The present paper preserves the original exploratory work and its motivation. Its further development can build on existing run artifacts, without making new experiments a prerequisite for documenting the idea.
