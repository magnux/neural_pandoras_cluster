\section{Connections to Denoising and Generative Dynamics}
\label{sec:related}

Two questions locate ReGAE among related approaches. What does a second model learn when its training data are the representations of a first model? And when a denoiser is called repeatedly, what does each prediction tell the sampler to do?

\subsection{From cellular growth to reusable denoising}

In Growing Neural Cellular Automata, the same local rule that grows a pattern from a seed can restore parts of it after damage~\cite{nca}. Neural Cellular Automata Manifold brings cellular growth into an autoencoder: an encoder selects the growth program that reconstructs an input~\cite{ncam}. Its code-mutation experiments perturb the program rather than the developing pattern. In ReGAE the decoder resumes its work on the damaged state itself, and its denoiser can use context beyond a local cellular neighborhood.

Denoising autoencoders offer another route from reconstruction to generation. In generalized denoising autoencoders, a generative chain alternates corruption with learned reconstruction~\cite{dae}. For some regularized autoencoders, the displacement from input to reconstruction is related to the score of the data density~\cite{alain}. A ReGAE decoder also defines a displacement, but it is trained here to restore a sample given its code. Interpreting that displacement as a density score would require additional assumptions about the loss and corruption process.

\subsection{What is being modeled in latent space?}

Diffusion Autoencoders (DiffAE) also separate reconstruction from the task of generating codes~\cite{diffae}. Their encoder supplies one semantic vector per image. Together with a spatial stochastic code, this vector allows a conditional diffusion decoder to reconstruct the image. To generate new semantic vectors, DiffAE trains a separate diffusion prior with a multilayer perceptron. The Pandora Gencoder instead learns from a spatial array of codes, each describing a different patch of the source image. Its task is to predict the array for a slightly displaced view. The decoder and the Gencoder use the same kind of denoiser, but only the decoder interprets a supplied code as a request for pixels.

Latent diffusion also generates images by modeling spatial tensors in an autoencoder's learned space~\cite{ldm}. SinGAN addresses the single-image problem from another direction, learning patch statistics at several image scales~\cite{singan}. In the Pandora case the change of scale comes from changing what serves as a training sample: a patch of pixels for the autoencoder becomes one element in a patch of codes for the Gencoder.

\subsection{Prediction targets and update rules}
\label{sec:targets}

A ReGAE denoiser predicts the state to use on its next call. A diffusion denoiser can also predict a clean state, although a common DDPM formulation predicts the noise added to the sample~\cite{ddpm,chan2024}. For a corrupted state $u_t=a_t x+b_t\epsilon$, where $a_t$ and $b_t$ are known coefficients, a noise estimate gives the corresponding clean-state estimate
\begin{equation}
 \widehat x(u_t,t)=\frac{u_t-b_t\widehat\epsilon_\theta(u_t,t)}{a_t},
 \qquad a_t\ne0.
 \label{eq:target-conversion}
\end{equation}
Converting between the estimates need not give the same training loss: the coefficients change the weight of errors at different times. For sampling, the key distinction is that a diffusion sampler uses the estimate to construct a transition to the next noise level. That transition can be deterministic, as in DDIM~\cite{ddim}. ReGAE can take the denoiser's output itself as the next state.

Flow matching learns a different quantity: a velocity $v_\theta(u,t)$ along a probability path~\cite{flowmatching}. As in neural ODEs~\cite{neuralode}, a solver integrates the learned derivative. Even one Euler step must specify how far to move: $u(t+h)\approx u(t)+h\,v_\theta(u(t),t)$. Score-based diffusion also admits an ODE sampler via its probability-flow formulation~\cite{sde}. Table~\ref{tab:targets} separates the quantity predicted from the operation used to advance the state.

\subsection{One correction map across corruption levels}
\label{sec:geometry}

Consider restarting a decoder on a partially damaged image. The intact pixels still agree with the code, while the damaged region may resemble noise. With its code held fixed, the ReGAE decoder applies the same displacement $\Delta_z(u)=D_\theta(u,z)-u$ whenever it encounters state $u$. It does not need a time label for the damage. A time-conditioned field $U(u,t)$ can prescribe different updates at the very same $u$, depending on $t$. Figure~\ref{fig:time-fields} illustrates the distinction.

The contours suggest a basin of attraction. For repair, that picture is justified locally if the decoder brings each perturbed state closer to a fixed reconstruction; Appendix~\ref{sec:geometry-notes} gives a sufficient condition. It also shows how to write a direct update as a unit Euler step without training a velocity field. The distinction matters for the Gencoder: its shifted target is meant to move to a new view. The video sampler can also refresh noise according to frame number even though the Gencoder itself receives no time input.
