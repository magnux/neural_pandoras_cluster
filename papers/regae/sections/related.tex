\section{Connections to Denoising and Generative Dynamics}
\label{sec:related}

The ReGAE construction sits at the intersection of iterative reconstruction and modeling in learned representation spaces. Its interpretation depends on four choices: what the network predicts, what conditions that prediction, how the output updates the state, and which relationships the state represents.

\subsection{From cellular growth to reusable denoising}

Growing NCA learns cellular updates that support growth, persistence, and recovery after damage~\cite{nca}. NCAM introduces an encoder-selected growth program for autoencoding~\cite{ncam}. Its code-mutation experiments establish a different form of robustness from a damaged-state recovery evaluation. ReGAE combines this autoencoding lineage with the regeneration objective, using a denoiser over an entire sample or latent window instead of requiring a strictly local cellular rule.

Iterative denoising also has a generative history independent of NCA. Generalized denoising autoencoders define chains that alternate corruption and reconstruction, with walkback training addressing unwanted modes~\cite{dae}. Regularized autoencoders admit connections to density scores under specified assumptions~\cite{alain}. These precedents matter: recurrence and state prediction alone do not establish novelty or a score interpretation. ReGAE proposes a particular reusable interface and studies its composition across representations; its loss and corruption model must be considered when making stronger probabilistic claims.

\subsection{Autoencoders and models of latent structure}

Diffusion Autoencoders (DiffAE) provide a close comparison~\cite{diffae}. They pair an image encoder with a conditional DDIM decoder, representing an image through a nonspatial semantic vector and a spatial stochastic code. They also learn a latent diffusion prior over semantic vectors using a multilayer perceptron. Latent diffusion models similarly perform generation in a learned autoencoder space~\cite{ldm}. Thus, adding a latent generator is not by itself the distinctive contribution of ReGAE.

In the Pandora composition, the higher-level model reuses the denoiser architecture family on spatial windows of patch codes, with a displaced prediction target. This makes relationships among encoded local samples the object of learning. SinGAN likewise shows the importance of multiscale structure in single-image modeling~\cite{singan}; the ReGAE case offers a representation-based construction for examining that problem, within a family that is not restricted to images.

\subsection{Prediction targets and update rules}
\label{sec:targets}

ReGAE directly assigns the predicted target state to the next iterate. A common DDPM formulation instead predicts noise~\cite{ddpm}, but diffusion also permits clean-state prediction~\cite{chan2024}. For the corruption model $u_t=a_t x+b_t\epsilon$, the estimates satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \widehat x(u_t,t)=\frac{u_t-b_t\widehat\epsilon_\theta(u_t,t)}{a_t},
 \qquad a_t\ne0.
 \label{eq:target-conversion}
\end{equation}
The conversion does not make differently weighted training losses identical. Nor does predicting a clean state require assigning that estimate directly at every step: a diffusion sampler uses a scheduled transition. DDIM gives a related construction permitting deterministic sampling~\cite{ddim}.

Flow Matching predicts a velocity along probability paths~\cite{flowmatching}. Its sampler integrates $du/dt=v_\theta(u,t)$, for example with an Euler step $u(t+h)\approx u(t)+h\,v_\theta(u(t),t)$. Neural ODEs also parameterize derivatives for integration~\cite{neuralode}, while score-based diffusion has a probability-flow ODE formulation~\cite{sde}. The useful distinction is the role of the learned output, rather than a requirement that other models use complicated solvers. Table~\ref{tab:targets} separates these choices.

\subsection{One correction map across corruption levels}
\label{sec:geometry}

At fixed code, ReGAE applies one displacement field $\Delta_z(u)$ wherever a state occurs. A time-conditioned field $U(u,t)$ can give different updates at that same state. Figure~\ref{fig:time-fields} visualizes the distinction using a common contour landscape. Colored bands associate stages with progress through the illustration; actual time-conditioned fields share the state domain, their state distributions can overlap, and time may be continuous~\cite{sde}. Omitting time provides one clock-free map, not a proof that the map is continuous or defined by a scalar potential.

The basin intuition is useful when the desired reconstruction is an attracting fixed point. Reconstruction training alone does not prove that property, and a diffusion or flow trajectory need not end at an equilibrium of its field. Appendix~\ref{sec:geometry-notes} states a sufficient local repair condition and shows how a direct update can be written algebraically as a unit Euler step, without identifying it with a trained probability-flow ODE. In Pandora, the shifted target intentionally creates progression between views. Its external noise-refresh schedule also depends on frame index, although the neural map itself receives no clock.
