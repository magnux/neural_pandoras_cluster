\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Regenerative neural dynamics.}
Growing Neural Cellular Automata distinguish growth, persistence, and regeneration, with damage during training strengthening recovery~\cite{nca}.
Neural Cellular Automata Manifold (NCAM) extends cellular growth to an autoencoding setting through dynamic convolutions~\cite{ncam}. Its reported code-mutation experiments concern robustness of the encoding; they do not constitute a damaged-state regeneration evaluation.
ReGAE continues this investigation through conditional denoising autoencoders and an unconditional variant that retains the regenerative operator without an encoder.
Their state inputs and outputs occupy the same domain and have matching dimensions, enabling repeated application.
The image implementation studied here uses U-Nets and attention beyond a strictly local cellular rule; these components and the optional latent hierarchy instantiate the broader construction.

\paragraph{Denoising as generative modeling.}
Bengio et al.~\cite{dae} formulate generative Markov chains through alternating corruption and denoising, including walkback training to address spurious modes.
Alain and Bengio~\cite{alain} connect regularized reconstruction to density scores under specified assumptions.
ReGAE permits different reconstruction objectives, including $L_1$, $L_2$, and learned-feature losses.
The present image application uses a VGG-based \emph{perceptive} loss, often termed a perceptual loss~\cite{vgg,perceptual}, while its latent predictor uses $L_1$.
These choices are application-specific; a score interpretation requires checking the corresponding corruption and loss assumptions.

\paragraph{Diffusion and continuous-time models.}
Diffusion probabilistic models learn to reverse a prescribed noise process~\cite{ddpm}; DDIM permits deterministic sampling through a related non-Markovian construction~\cite{ddim}.
Score-based SDEs yield a probability-flow ODE with matching time-marginal distributions under the corresponding score field~\cite{sde}.
Neural ODEs parameterize continuous-time derivatives and integrate them with differential-equation solvers~\cite{neuralode}.
Flow Matching learns a time-dependent velocity field along probability paths~\cite{flowmatching}. Chan's tutorial connects signal and noise prediction with numerical sampling, providing a useful account of these geometric relationships~\cite{chan2024}.
ReGAE directly predicts desired states without explicit noise-time conditioning. Sections~\ref{sec:direct-map} and~\ref{sec:geometry} distinguish this update from noise prediction and velocity integration, without treating any one parameterization as universal to diffusion.

\paragraph{Autoencoding and latent generative structure.}
Diffusion Autoencoders (DiffAE) are particularly close to ReGAE's conditional autoencoding formulation~\cite{diffae}.
They combine a learned semantic encoder with a conditional DDIM decoder, representing an image through a 512-dimensional nonspatial semantic vector and a spatial stochastic code.
Both parts contribute to near-exact reconstruction.
DiffAE also fits a latent DDIM prior over semantic vectors using a deep multilayer perceptron with skip connections.
Latent diffusion models likewise perform generative denoising in an autoencoder's representation space~\cite{ldm}.
In our Pandora's Cluster application, an unconditional ReGAE, the Gencoder, models spatial windows of patch codes and predicts displaced encodings.
This spatial prediction hierarchy is one composition of the family, and differs from DiffAE's prior over image-level semantic vectors.

\paragraph{Learning from a single image.}
SinGAN learns multiscale patch distributions from one natural image~\cite{singan}.
Our image case study similarly examines local appearance and spatial organization, using encoded patches and displaced prediction.
The setting makes the distinction between reconstructing a particular arrangement and generating familiar-looking content visible, without restricting ReGAE to single-image modeling.

\paragraph{Representation and compression.}
Minimum description length counts both model and data descriptions~\cite{mdl}.
COIN stores an image through quantized coordinate-network weights~\cite{coin}, while language-model compression connects predictive distributions to explicit coding~\cite{lmcompression}.
These perspectives inform the interpretation of ReGAE representations.
An operational compression claim additionally requires accounting for precision, model storage, residual information, and reconstruction distortion.
