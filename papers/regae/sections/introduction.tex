\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

What must a model learn in order to reconstruct a dataset? Even when its behavior is described as memorization, a mechanism is still needed to represent the samples, recover their details, and capture relationships among them. These requirements need not be met by the same learned object. An autoencoder may reconstruct every sample when given its code, while leaving the distribution or organization of those codes to be supplied externally. Asking where this remaining information resides turns memorization into a constructive modeling problem.

Our aim is to investigate how a learning system can first model data and then make its own learned representations objects of further modeling. We pursue this through \emph{Regenerative Autoencoders} (ReGAE), a family built around a reusable reconstruction process. A conditional ReGAE combines an encoder with a denoiser that acts on the current state of a sample. The denoiser predicts a corrected state directly, and its output can be supplied as its next input. It receives a latent code but no iteration index or corruption-level label. An unconditional member retains the state-update operator without the encoder-derived code.

This construction connects generation and repair. Starting from noise asks the operator to construct a sample; restarting from a damaged reconstruction asks it to restore that sample. Neural cellular automata use the biological term \emph{regeneration} for this latter behavior, distinguishing it from initial growth and persistence~\cite{nca}. Neural Cellular Automata Manifold (NCAM) supplies the autoencoding lineage, in which an encoder selects a cellular growth program for reconstruction~\cite{ncam}. ReGAE develops that lineage with a shape-preserving denoiser that can be reused on data or on learned representations.

The second use is central to the research question. Once a model has formed a representation of its data, another member of the same family can learn regularities within that representation. We use \emph{meta-modeling} for this process: making the structure expressed in a model's learned representations the subject of another model. Within the composite ReGAE system, it is a form of \emph{representation-level self-modeling}. The object being modeled is the encoder's output, and the two levels have separate learned parameters. Reusing the same modeling interface makes the construction recursively extensible. The longer-term hypothesis is that such meta-modeling can expose further relationships within a system's internal models and contribute to more general adaptive intelligence. Section~\ref{sec:recursive-discussion} develops this motivation and its scope.

Pandora's Cluster provides a worked example. We treat patches from one large astronomical image as samples. A conditional ReGAE learns their representations and reconstruction; a second, unconditional ReGAE learns spatial relationships among the resulting codes. This latent model, the \emph{Gencoder}, predicts a slightly displaced view, allowing its recurrent updates to generate extended trajectories through image content. The arrangement makes an otherwise abstract distinction visible: knowing how patches look and knowing how their representations fit together are different parts of modeling the image.

The two spatial levels are an application, not the definition of ReGAE. In a collection of separate images, the first level could encode complete images and a subsequent model could address the distribution or other relationships among their codes. More generally, the representation and the relationships supplied during training determine what another level can learn. Our objective is to make this reusable design explicit and inspect its behavior, rather than to claim a record on a particular benchmark.

The paper contributes three connected elements: the conditional and unconditional state-correction formulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}); a concrete composition that models encoder-produced fields in the Pandora case (Sec.~\ref{sec:evidence}); and an account of its connections to generative dynamics and recursive meta-modeling (Secs.~\ref{sec:related}--\ref{sec:discussion}). The evidence consists of the archived implementation, preserved training records, and the author's qualitative observations from the original experiments. This scope supports an exploratory account of the construction and motivates questions about what further levels of modeling would capture.
