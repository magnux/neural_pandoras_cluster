# Writing ReGAE: from reconstructing a sample to modeling the encoder

Editorial guide for the next revision · November 25, 2026 · Based on the [v0.8 paper](../papers/regae/regae.pdf) and the [20-slide research talk](../presentations/regae/regae.pdf).

**The sentence the reader should be able to say back:** ReGAE uses a denoiser to reconstruct a sample and to resume reconstruction after damage. On Pandora’s Cluster, a second denoiser learns to predict what the first model’s encoder will write for a neighboring view.

That sentence has two claims. The first defines a **family of models**; the second describes one use of it. Keep them distinct. The larger ambition is to understand how a system can learn about the data by modeling its own responses to them. The experiment gives that ambition a concrete prediction target. It does not require a record score, an exact copy of the mosaic, or a verdict on whether neural networks can extrapolate.

## The plot for the next draft

1. **Begin with a reason to reconstruct.** If one aim of intelligence is to extract and organize the information in a dataset, how would we tell what a model retained? Two images can both earn the correct label “galaxy.” Asking the model to render *this* galaxy makes their differences matter. Pose this as a way to investigate learning, rather than a definition of intelligence or “grokking.”
2. **Move one tile.** Slide a tile into the empty square of the galaxy puzzle. Every star printed on the tile survives; a spiral arm that once joined two edges now breaks. The reader can see what patch reconstruction measures and what it leaves out. Saving all the patch codes **in their original order** supplies the missing arrangement. Now there is a reason to ask what a model can learn about that arrangement.
3. **Explain regeneration with one patch.** The encoder reads the reference patch once and writes a code. The decoder reads that fixed code and a mutable image state, producing the next image state directly. Start from noise to generate; give it a damaged reconstruction to resume. The image goes around the loop. The encoder does not. Introduce “regeneration” in ordinary language here; give growing NCA and NCAM their historical credit in the introduction.
4. **Let the encoder’s answers become data.** Freeze the trained encoder. A separate denoiser, the *gencoder*, reads a corrupted window of its codes and predicts the codes for a slightly displaced view. The bottleneck is a deliberate place to observe the encoder: its response is compact, and we can decode it to see what it describes. We could have chosen another layer or, on another dataset, another relationship. *Meta-modeling* names the choice to learn those responses. Reapplying a decoder to its own output is *recurrence*; only the former trains a second model.
5. **End with what remains unresolved.** The author reports coherent stretches of sky, sometimes followed by an apparent jump. A locally plausible continuation can lose the source location. Return to the puzzle with a sharper question: could a *sequence* of views locate a region that a single view cannot? That gives a possible next level something specific to predict. Only two levels were trained.

| **§1 · Introduction** | Move from the information question to reconstruction, the tile, and the repair loop. Briefly name the lineage. If not, move it to the method or cut it. |
| **§2 · Method** | Define sample, code, and state in the order the reader encounters them. An *encoder* and a *denoiser* are architectures; *decoder* is the denoiser’s role when it interprets a code, and *gencoder* is its role when it generates codes. Use lowercase in running text. A denoiser without an external code is not automatically a gencoder. The denoiser preserves the shape of a 1D, 2D, or 3D state. Pandora uses a U-shaped network, a VGG perceptual loss, and two spatial levels. Another application could use a different network or an L1 or L2 reconstruction loss. |
| **§3 · Pandora case study** | First explain why the patch objective leaves placement unspecified. Then show the shift *before* describing the sampler. Slide 9 offers a usable sentence: “The star stays put; when the crop origin moves eight pixels down and right, the star moves from (27, 14) to (19, 6) in crop coordinates.” This is a schematic example. In v2 the shift is about a quarter patch, before encoding, so even the overlap needs newly aligned codes. During video sampling the latent canvas persists; the pixel decoder renders each final code in one call from fresh noise. |
| **§4 · Related models and dynamics** | Compare what a model predicts, what conditions it, and how the sampler uses the prediction. A ReGAE output is the next state. Diffusion can predict noise or a clean-state estimate; flow matching predicts velocity. Distinguish the content code from a timestep. For DiffAE, the relevant contrast is its prior on one semantic vector per image versus a displaced spatial window of codes here. DiffAE also has a spatial stochastic reconstruction code. |
| **§5 · Discussion and §6 · Conclusion** | Give the code-as-program interpretation its payoff here. Separate local fidelity from plausible detail and plausible layout from the original address. Discuss further recursion, including its possible relevance to AGI, as the author’s hypothesis. Close on the unresolved location question rather than a general promise. |
| **Appendices** | Put exact architecture, gate, losses, schedules, storage assumptions, mathematical conditions, and archived traces where readers can inspect them. Keep “neural black holes” a brief historical recollection if used. The compression essay and NVD paper have [their own scope](publication-roadmap.md). |

## Write the code-as-program passage around one decision

The encoder writes a code $z$ for one patch. The decoder weights are shared across patches. For fixed weights, selecting $z$ selects the update rule $T_z(u)=D_\theta(u,z)$. This is what it means to *run the patch program*: apply that rule to the current state. The code’s meaning comes from the decoder that interprets it; the analogy does not require a symbolic language or a claim about algorithmic complexity.

Use the faint star from slide 6. One design could spend encoder capacity and code coordinates to specify its position and brightness, leaving a simple decoder to draw it. Another could use a simple encoder and a short code to select a galaxy profile that a richer decoder knows how to render. If moving the star gives the same code, then, from the same starting state, the decoder cannot tell which position was observed. Fresh noise can supply convincing texture. It cannot tell the decoder which star was there. Now the engineering choice has a purpose: preserve a one-pixel displacement when it matters, or ignore a nuisance fluctuation when stability matters. Width, precision, weights, loss, and noise all affect the result. These are design alternatives, not a capacity comparison run on Pandora.

Use **compression** for two distinct questions. The network maps a structured $32\times32\times3$ patch to 256 coordinates: 3,072 to 256, a twelvefold reduction in dimensionality. Its real-valued input here comes from 8-bit samples. Counting 8-bit RGB against an FP32 code gives 3,072 versus 1,024 payload bytes, before the model and placement information. Neither comparison establishes a twelvefold file saving or a measured rate at matched fidelity. Further coding of the latent is outside the study. Credit [Sanderson’s Part 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbsb9DpdZ6s) for the lossless message-and-decoder accounting; interpreting the latent itself as a program is the ReGAE argument. Introduce that thought in §2, develop it in §5, and leave the accounting in the appendix.

## Before accepting a paragraph

- **Name the actor and the object.** Replace “the model learns itself” with “the gencoder predicts what the frozen encoder will write for shifted crops.” Reusing the architecture does not share weights or recover an encoder’s parameters. Use *meta-modeling*, not *meta-learning*.
- **Choose a verb the evidence supports.** The notebook *implements* a sampler; its saved outputs *record* training losses; the author *recalls* coherent views, VHS-like texture, and jumps; ambiguous local content *could explain* the jumps. Local position information exists inside the window, but the gencoder receives no global mosaic address. The displayed statistic is mean squared latent activation, not entropy. The [evidence inventory](evidence-inventory.md) is the reference for stronger wording or for adding recovered media.
- **Give the reader the consequence.** “The same code can be reused” is less informative than “Damage the image, keep the code, and restart the decoder.” That explains the available mechanism. It does not turn the historical video into a controlled repair test, or the mixed v0 notebook into a clean baseline. No new experiments are required for this account.

**Writing references retained from the original plan.** [Mensh & Kording](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619) and [Peyton Jones](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf) motivate the single through-line and intuition before machinery. [Widom](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/widom/paper-writing.html) informs section order; [Gopen & Swan](https://courses.ems.psu.edu/styleforstudents/print/c10_p6.html) motivate introducing familiar context before a new term. [Dahlstrom](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1320645111) and the [Nature Methods editorial on storytelling](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth.2726) inform how we use an example without mistaking its vividness for evidence. These are editorial choices for this paper, not experimental findings about ReGAE.
